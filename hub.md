## The brief

### The ask

How might our relationship with the ocean change by 2040, and what could that mean for education?

- What might our relationship with the ocean look and feel like in 2040?
- What might shape or disrupt that relationship?
- What could an immersive educational experience look like in that future?
- We depend on the ocean for food, livelihoods, trade, energy and climate regulation, yet global warming, pollution, overfishing and habitat destruction keep the pressure on.

Source: Brief deck (OceanX, Mar 2026)


### What success looks like

A future world brought to life through artefacts or prototypes the team can test and get feedback on.

- Evidence that learning and education can contribute to positive impact for the ocean
- Scale, both in reach and in depth of knowledge
- Current work moves toward transdisciplinary areas, including blue economy and blue futures
- Stakeholders: the team decides and identifies them

Source: Brief deck (OceanX, Mar 2026)

### The stakes

The ocean covers most of the planet and produces much of our oxygen, yet we’ve barely seen it.

- 71% of Earth is covered by ocean
- About 50% of Earth’s oxygen comes from phytoplankton
- Less than 5% of the ocean directly explored; about 0.001% of the deep seafloor observed directly
- Less than 30% of the ocean floor mapped (28.7% in 2026)

Source: Brief deck (OceanX, Mar 2026)

## Ship and tools

### The OceanXplorer

A floating laboratory, filmmaking studio and deep-sea launchpad.

- 87 m long, up to 72 people aboard
- Two Triton submersibles, Neptune (science) and Nadir (media), to 1,000 m
- Argus ROV system to 6,000 m
- Mission control (OpenVDM) integrates data from acoustic array, subs, ROV and CTD in real time
- Wet lab, dry labs with eDNA sequencing and microscopy, helicopter, dive center, media production center, Otter uncrewed surface vehicle

Source: oceanx.org/oceanxplorer
Link: https://oceanx.org/oceanxplorer/

### Science tools explained

The instruments behind the data, from the brief’s appendix.

- Acoustic mapping: sound waves measure seafloor depth
- CTD sensor: conductivity, temperature and depth through the water column, plus water samples
- eDNA: DNA shed by organisms, sequenced on board to identify species
- ROV: piloted via cable, robotic arms, lasers 10 cm apart for scale
- Autonomous surface vehicle: sonar, LiDAR and cameras for reefs, glaciers and coasts

Source: Brief deck (OceanX, Mar 2026)

### Expeditions

Recent OceanXplorer missions.

- Cabo Verde: Digital Deep 2025 (first digital twin concept)
- Around Africa 2025
- Indonesia, Malaysia and Seychelles 2024
- United Arab Emirates and Azores 2023; Red Sea 2022

Source: oceanx.org/oceanxplorer
Link: https://oceanx.org/missions/

### Immersive Development Toolkit

Open resources for students, educators and developers building ocean education experiences, aimed at hackathon participants and education partners.

- Real mission data: bathymetry, eDNA samples, CTD readings, ROV footage
- 3D models, textures and source files, including marine species, coral and the OceanXplorer itself
- 2 mission datasets, 50+ 3D assets, 100+ video clips, 10+ expeditions

## Education

### Education objectives and ecosystem

Three objectives, four program areas.

- Objectives: ocean literacy, ocean career pathways, ocean-positive decision-making
- Leadership and talent development: Young Explorers, Early Career Explorers, hackathons, fellowships, residencies
- Classroom and educator experiences
- Immersive and digital experiences: films, exhibitions, interactive media

Source: Brief deck (OceanX, Mar 2026)

### Leadership immersions and fellowship

Small, selective, deep programmes on and around the ship.

- Young Explorers, 18–24: multi-day immersion aboard; 14 picked from thousands of applicants (2024)
- Early Career Explorers, 25+ with up to five years of experience
- Ocean Futures Fellowship, 21+ with a bachelor degree: six months, remote, with IIE; pilot cohort of three
- Art and Science Residency for artists and scientists

Source: Deck C: as-is state
Confidence: Documented

### Hackathons and the learner pathway

Explore, Create, Prototype, Continue. Hackathons are “often” the entry point, but movement between steps isn’t measured.

- About 15 participants per hackathon, on the ship and onshore, using real mission data
- Concepts continue into the Education Impact Challenge, shown at the Summit and ArtScience Museum
- The journey ends at an undefined “Continue”; alumni network in development since 2023

Source: Combined synthesis
Confidence: Medium

### Classroom, digital and public access

How OceanX reaches people who never board.

- Education booklets in English and Bahasa Indonesia, curriculum-aligned
- Educators’ workshops across all subjects
- Livestreams from submersibles to classrooms; ship tours
- Into the Ocean: Journey Beneath at Singapore’s ArtScience Museum
- SeaSwipe: the public labels species to train AI models

## Evidence

### Reach and scale: what is reported

Units differ and none of these figures measure learning outcomes.

- 570+ students and educators aboard in Singapore since 2024
- 223 students and educators at the 2026 Summit (of 750+ participants)
- 100,000+ learners via livestreams and media (self-reported, undated)
- 1,610 ship-tour visitors; 83+ trained through leadership immersions
- About 0.6M YouTube subscribers

Source: Combined synthesis
Confidence: Self-reported

### From wonder to responsibility

Reach and knowledge don’t reliably turn into action, and the one finding on what does is thin.

- Responsibility (0.51) predicts pro-ocean behaviour better than literacy (0.39) or values (0.13); one survey, 1,206 Chinese undergraduates
- Review of 102 papers: knowledge-behaviour link “surprisingly low”
- Blue Planet II: aware viewers reported stronger intent (78% vs 37% on single-use plastic), but in a test real choices didn’t change
- Ages 16–25 across 10 countries: about 6 in 10 say governments are failing; 3 in 4 find the future frightening
- Hope vs fear framing: no clear winner

Source: Deck B: evidence and 2040
Confidence: Low-Medium

### How comparable organisations measure

Almost everyone counts outputs, not behaviour change.

- NatGeo Pristine Seas: 26 of 35 places since protected, but designation isn’t protection
- Schmidt Ocean Institute: free ship time, open data, no quantitative KPIs
- Mission Blue: 170+ Hope Spots; protection depends on others
- Ocean Conservancy: 19M+ volunteers, counts pounds not behaviour
- Monterey Bay Aquarium: no measured change in visitor behaviour
- Coral Gardeners: youth-led, 250K+ corals planted

Source: Combined synthesis

### Why OceanX’s science wins matter

Real discoveries, with little documented follow-through.

- Seabed mapped globally: 6% in 2017, 28.7% in 2026; OceanX feeds the open map
- Five uncharted seamounts found in Indonesia (2024); no follow-on protection documented
- Cabo Verde digital twin aimed at 30x30 planning; no independent evaluation found
- Roo Rise high-seas expedition; link to policy is our inference

Source: Deck C: as-is state

### Six flows between the actors

Money, access, data, content, people and trust.

- Money: parent funds the program; partner amounts mostly undisclosed
- Access: research in a state’s waters needs that state’s consent
- Data: results owed to coastal states; some data can’t be shared (Indonesia)
- Content: footage reaches the public via partner media
- People: hackathon to Challenge to prototypes
- Trust: UNESCO Ocean Decade framing, university and museum partners

Source: Combined synthesis

### Three frameworks for reading the ecosystem

Lenses, not findings.

- Business ecosystem (Moore, 1993): who sits inside, next to and around the client
- Stakeholder salience (Mitchell, Agle and Wood, 1997): who holds power, legitimacy and urgency
- Resource dependence (Pfeffer and Salancik, 1978): where influence could enter through money, permits, data, distribution

## Ocean outlook

### Ocean threats, and where learners can act

The threats with the strongest data aren’t always the ones learners can act on.

- Warming: record heat in 2025, reefs 84% stressed
- Acidity up 17% since 1985
- 37.7% of fish stocks overfished
- Plastic: 130 Mt now, 280 Mt by 2040 if nothing changes, 47 Mt with system change; 10 of the top 20 emitting countries are in Asia
- Deep-sea mining: ISA rules deferred to March 2027; 45 countries want a pause
- Protection: 10% designated, about 3.3% effectively protected
- Sea-level rise 3.8–4.2 mm a year

Source: Deck B: evidence and 2040
Confidence: Strong data

### Forces around the client

Social, technological, economic, environmental and political drivers.

- Social: ocean missing from many curricula; Black scientists under 3% of US ocean scientists
- Technological: AI annotation, digital twins, immersive media and XR, citizen science set to expand
- Economic: ocean philanthropy about $1B a year; SDG 14 is the least funded goal; ocean economy about 40M jobs by 2030
- Political: High Seas Treaty in force January 2026; 30x30 about 20 points short; mining moving to permits

Source: Combined synthesis

## 2040 futures

### What we can state about 2040

Most projections run to 2030, 2050 or 2100, so 2040 sits between them. Anchors, not predictions.

- 2030: 30x30 deadline, ocean economy about 40M jobs, full seabed map targeted
- 2040: plastic 280 Mt if nothing changes; today’s 18–25s are 32–39; few direct ocean projections
- 2050: Arctic ice-free in September at least once; US coast up 10–12 inches; marine heatwaves keep increasing

Source: Deck B: evidence and 2040

### Four futures from two critical uncertainties

Does governance deliver? Does learning turn into action?

- Shared stewardship: governance delivers, action gap closes
- People ahead of policy: governance lags, action gap closes
- Rules without people: governance delivers, action gap persists
- Drift: governance lags, action gap persists
- Focus areas 2, 3 and 5 hold in all four futures

Source: Deck B: evidence and 2040
Confidence: Judgement

## Problems and gaps

### Six problems to explore

Problems, not solutions. Provisional until the client validates the baseline.

- P1. Depth is tied to a ship, so access is rationed (strongest)
- P2. No defined destination after the hackathon
- P3. Can’t show whether the education work changes anything
- P4. Audience defined differently in different places; no user research
- P5. Mass reach depends on partners; nothing links content to learning
- P6. Learning material depends on permissions OceanX doesn’t control
